When I started reading "The Sun Eater" series, I knew I could be in for a literary journey. As I write this, seven installments in this saga by Christopher Ruocchio have been published. I think the debut is quite good.

The series' main character is Hadrian – a member of the nobility of one of the most important families in this universe. We learn about his adventures in high politics, as well as his growing up process and the life and moral choices he makes. The plot of "Empire of Silence" is, in my opinion, the weakest element of the book. Due to the author's chosen structure, we encounter a very long flashback. Hadrian tells his story and describes the process by which he became the titular "Sun Eater."

A lot happens in this installment. Probably too much, in my opinion. Many of the storylines are short and illogical. I feel like they were squeezed in by the author to loosely connect the subsequent stages of the protagonist's galactic journey. Many chapters were simply difficult to read, and it was difficult to get through the pages of the teenage protagonist's musings or descriptions of gladiatorial training.

What I love most is the vast and vibrant world presented in the book. Already in this installment, we learn about many planets, clans, cities, and elements of local mythology that serve as the backdrop for the plot. This isn't hard science fiction, so the emphasis is on presenting the political systems, history, social relationships, and the diversity of languages ​​and cultures in this universe. It's a good idea to systematize this knowledge in the form of additional chapters at the end of the book, after the main storyline is completed.

Although the long sections were challenging, I will definitely be reading the next installments in the Sun Eater series. The expansive and vibrant world created in the first installment encourages further exploration.
