This short novella is the author's commentary on imperialism, environmentalism, and the expansive nature of our civilization. The book was written in 1972, and the ongoing Vietnam War certainly had a significant impact on its content.

Technological advancement has brought humanity to a remote forest planet inhabited by meter-tall, peaceful humanoid natives. Upon arriving, humanity gradually begins to steal the locals' most valuable resource (timber), and conflicts between the two races escalate. The embodiment of injustice and injustice at the hands of humanity is the main antihero, Davidson.

His character is utterly repulsive. At the other extreme are the local tribe of Athsheans, who must contend with humanity's brutality and colonialist tendencies.

The author's message is clear: humanity is capable of destruction, exploitation, and maximizing profit at the expense of nature and other civilizations. Le Guin clearly outlined her negative attitude toward war and imperialism. There's a stark contrast between humans and the nonviolent Aestheans.

The book is short, which keeps the action very fast-paced and free from unnecessary plot threads.

It's a compelling commentary on reality, presented in an engaging science fiction format.
