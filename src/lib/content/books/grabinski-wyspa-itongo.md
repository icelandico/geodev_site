A novel that begins eerily and mysteriously, but ends amateurishly and thoughtlessly.

I felt like I was reading two different stories. The beginning is very promising. The atmosphere is very fitting for Lovecraft's and Grabiński's stories – an abandoned house, an oppressive and uncertain atmosphere, and the psychic elements the main character discovers within himself. I had hoped this thread would permeate the rest of the novel, but I was very surprised.

When the main character arrives on the titular Itongo Island, what had happened previously in his life becomes irrelevant. From a horror novel, the plot transforms into a study of a white man's discovery of a primitive civilization living in an uncharted area of ​​the Pacific. The white man was quickly promoted to the rank of chief by the local population. From that point on, the book depicts how our main character manages his estate on an uncharted island.

It's important to take into account the time of the novel's writing (the 1930s) and the different perceptions of Europeans at the time of less developed cultures. Perhaps the author's reflections on colonialism and the then-popular attempts by authorities to acquire overseas possessions are contained here.

"Itongo Island" differs significantly from Grabiński's other work. It lacks much in the way of horror, suspense, or paranormal elements. Despite this, I consider it a successful adventure read. Changing the novel's setting to such an exotic setting was a refreshing take on the work of this remarkable author.
