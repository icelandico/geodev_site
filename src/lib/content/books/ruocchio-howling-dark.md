I approached the second part of the "Sun Eater" saga with some reserve. While I enjoyed the first book, it didn’t quite elicit the same level of awe in me that I observed in other readers' reviews and comments.

In terms of the plot, it is, of course, a direct continuation of the previous volume. However, a gap of many years separates the two. By the end of "Empire of Silence," the protagonist is still a teenager; by the time "Howling Dark" begins, he is over 30. Together with his companions, he sets out in search of the mysterious, forgotten planet of Vorgossos, hoping to make contact with the aggressive and hostile Cielcin race. That’s the gist of it—though a great deal happens in this installment. And therein lies my primary issue with Ruocchio’s work.
This simply isn’t the kind of literature I prefer. I love science fiction—explorations of time travel, the impact of technology on society, or dystopian visions of what a future totalitarian state might look like.

This book contains a little bit of everything. I cannot deny the author’s grand ambition in world-building; the setting is vast and truly impressive. It features countless planets, a massive cast of characters, distant worlds, and alien races with their own unique languages ​​and cultures. For the particularly curious, there is even a glossary detailing the various noble houses and a chronology of key events within the universe. From my perspective as a reader, however, it’s just too much. The book is heavily focused on adventure—in that sense, the way the narrative unfolds could even be compared to the "Harry Potter" series. There is an immense amount going on. The narrative shifts dynamically and frequently between planets, political meetings, and consultations aboard spaceships. In my view, the author’s storytelling is overly long-winded, repetitive, and—in many extended passages—quite boring. Nevertheless, the central plot is intriguing enough that I will pick up the third volume, though I suspect this will be the last time I engage with this author’s work.

I am neither thrilled by this series nor put off by it. I happen to be the type of reader who finishes a book regardless of how I feel while reading it. And although getting through this book was a struggle for the most part, the author deserves credit for the vast, coherent world he has crafted in the *Sun Eater* saga.
