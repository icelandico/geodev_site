Another novella, and again in the science fiction genre. This time, it's set in a post-apocalyptic setting, though to be precise, the book's plot takes place during an apocalypse.

The novel's characters listen to disturbing news from Asia, where a virus is spreading that kills grass and crops. Social unrest grows, mass famine ensues, and millions die. When the plague finally reach Europe, the group heads north to find a safe haven during the crisis. The sudden change in life's circumstances causes the characters to change.

The need to adapt to new and uncomfortable conditions often requires a test of humanity on their part. And this, in my opinion, is the novel's main theme. The "catastrophe" itself is merely the backdrop, and the reader observes how ordinary people, not long ago engaged in ordinary lives, must fight for survival, food, shelter for the night, and their very lives. I enjoy novellas and short stories, but in this case, the book is too short. The action unfolds very quickly, with plot threads appearing and disappearing within a few pages.

Let's return to the characters. In my opinion, they are very broadly drawn and are meant to represent certain attitudes. So, we have archetypes like the cynic, the idealist, and the stupid muscleman. The characters are one-dimensional and uninteresting.

This is a relatively old book (written in the 1950s), and "The Death of Grass" fits, in my opinion, the style of science fiction novels of the time. The book may be quite naive and contains many simplifications, but the message remains unchanged. A person facing a serious crisis can undergo a metamorphosis and reveal their dark side.
